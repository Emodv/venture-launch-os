# Emod Marketing Doctrine

## Purpose
Distill repeatable marketing judgment from years of historical SEO, PPC, analytics, conversion, migration, reporting, and client-operating evidence into a reusable VLA decision layer.

This is not a biography and not a dump of private client data. It is an evidence-derived operating doctrine: how to diagnose, prioritize, act, report, and learn.

## Core principle
Marketing activity is not the objective. The objective is qualified demand, conversion, revenue, and durable business value.

Use the smallest number of high-leverage actions needed to move the business outcome.

## 1. Access before diagnosis
Before making major recommendations, get the historical truth.

Preferred evidence:
- Google Analytics / GA4
- Google Search Console
- Google Ads
- CRM / lead-quality data
- landing-page and call-tracking data
- CMS / site architecture
- ranking and backlink history
- prior campaign reports and strategic decisions

Do not confuse account access with data validity. Verify that the property/account is actually connected, active, and collecting the expected data.

If historical access is missing, state what is known publicly and what remains unverified.

## 2. Preserve proven equity before changing architecture
Before migration, redesign, CMS change, domain consolidation, or URL restructuring:

1. Identify pages already ranking organically.
2. Identify pages receiving backlinks and referral value.
3. Identify high-value landing pages and conversion paths.
4. Preserve or deliberately migrate those assets.
5. Stage changes before switching the production domain when possible.
6. Use explicit redirects/canonicals/internal-link mapping.
7. Verify analytics and conversion tracking after the change.

Never treat an existing site as disposable merely because a cleaner rebuild is possible.

## 3. Separate acquisition problems from conversion problems
When leads fall, do not assume traffic acquisition failed.

Diagnose the chain:

`impression -> click -> landing page -> interaction -> lead -> qualified lead -> sale`

Examples of diagnostic questions:
- Is traffic still arriving?
- Did CPC or impression share materially change?
- Are the same landing pages receiving traffic?
- Are phone numbers/forms/tracking still working?
- Is the page converting poorly despite healthy traffic?
- Did audience or geography quality change?

If traffic is healthy but leads are weak, prioritize landing-page, offer, UX, tracking, or lead-routing fixes before increasing spend.

## 4. Let real intent data cross-pollinate channels
Use SEO evidence to inform PPC and PPC evidence to inform SEO.

Patterns:
- organic ranking keywords -> candidate paid-search tests
- strong paid search terms -> candidate SEO/AEO service/content opportunities
- expensive but high-converting paid terms -> potential organic moat
- high-volume organic traffic with weak paid conversion evidence -> possible vanity traffic
- high-converting landing-page patterns -> candidates for broader organic/service expansion

Treat cross-channel transfer as a hypothesis, then verify.

## 5. Optimize to business quality, not traffic volume
Prefer qualified outcomes over vanity metrics.

Primary questions:
- Did lead volume improve?
- Did lead quality improve?
- Did cost per qualified opportunity improve?
- Did revenue or forecasted pipeline improve?
- Did the campaign reach the right geography/audience?

Traffic, clicks, CPC, rankings, and impressions are diagnostic metrics, not final success metrics.

## 6. Geography is an economic lever
Do not treat all regions equally.

Use actual performance to:
- exclude low-value geographies
- bid more aggressively in proven markets
- build localized landing pages where commercial value warrants it
- align ad spend with sales/service coverage

Geographic expansion follows evidence, not uniform national coverage by default.

## 7. Build dedicated landing pages for distinct intent
When a product, service, location, machine, audience, or offer has distinct intent, prefer a dedicated landing page over forcing all traffic into a generic page.

A strong landing page should align:
- query / ad intent
- headline / promise
- proof
- CTA
- geography
- conversion mechanism

Do not scale traffic into a page known not to convert.

## 8. Mobile-first is an operating requirement
When mobile represents the majority of traffic, mobile usability is the primary design constraint.

Check:
- table/component behavior
- CTA visibility
- load speed
- form friction
- tap targets
- content hierarchy
- conversion flow

Desktop quality does not compensate for a broken mobile experience.

## 9. Fix fatal technical issues before incremental SEO
Prioritize issues that can suppress the entire program:
- noindex on high-value pages
- robots blocking
- broken redirects
- wrong or disconnected analytics
- duplicate live/staging sites
- broken conversion tracking
- severe mobile usability failures
- plugin conflicts / unsafe site changes
- crawl/indexation failures

Only after fatal constraints are controlled should VLA scale content, links, or keyword coverage.

## 10. Prefer safe, reversible implementation
Before installing plugins, changing site architecture, or deploying technical SEO changes, assess compatibility and downside risk.

If expert feedback materially contradicts a proposed tactic, pause and re-evaluate rather than forcing the change through.

Avoid simultaneous overlapping tools/plugins that create conflicts.

## 11. Use phased roadmaps
A practical plan should distinguish immediate fixes from growth work.

Default sequencing:

### 0–30 days
- access / tracking
- fatal technical fixes
- baseline
- audience / keyword / competitor diagnosis
- landing-page and conversion fixes

### 31–60 days
- content/service expansion
- paid-search optimization
- authority building
- retargeting / CRM integration

### 61–90 days
- scale proven segments
- expand winning geographies / audiences
- automate reporting / lead handling
- double down on proven patterns

The dates are not rigid; the sequence is.

## 12. Demand operational visibility from vendors and teams
Reports must reveal what was actually done and what changed.

Avoid opaque vendor management.

Require:
- actions completed
- pages/campaigns affected
- before/after metrics
- expected outcome
- observed outcome
- next recommendation

If a vendor cannot explain what was done, what moved, or why the work should continue, re-evaluate the spend.

## 13. Cut or pause low-conviction spend
Do not continue a service because it is already in motion.

Pause, reduce, or cancel when:
- promised systems are not functioning
- reports are not informative
- no meaningful movement is visible
- work is not understood by the responsible operator
- the economics no longer justify continuation

Historical spend is not a reason to preserve future spend.

## 14. Test before scaling
Use controlled pilots to reduce risk.

Examples:
- low-bid keyword campaign to test lead quality
- geography test
- landing-page redesign test
- paid pilot before larger budget
- staged migration before domain switch
- always-on campaign only after strong campaign evidence

Ask for direct feedback on lead quantity and quality after the test.

## 15. Reporting doctrine: decision-first
A useful report should answer:

1. What happened?
2. Is it good or bad relative to target/baseline?
3. Why did it happen?
4. What does it mean for the business?
5. What are we changing next?
6. What outcome should that change produce?

Preferred structure:

`Finding -> Meaning -> Decision -> Action -> Expected Outcome`

Use forecasts when they help indicate whether the month/quarter is on track, but label forecasts as forecasts.

## 16. Track funnel quality, not just top-line leads
When possible, track stages such as:
- requested demo
- lead
- marketing-qualified / scored lead
- sales-qualified opportunity
- customer
- revenue

A smaller high-scoring segment can be more valuable than a larger low-quality one.

## 17. Clean, simple, direct UX
For performance-oriented pages, prefer layouts that are:
- clean
- simple
- immediately understandable
- comparison-friendly when relevant
- focused on the decision

Avoid visual complexity that weakens conversion clarity.

## 18. Content quality over mechanically generated text
Reject unreadable content created by synonym substitution or automation that degrades quality.

Content should be useful, readable, audience-aligned, and capable of supporting conversion or authority.

## 19. Scale what works; do not worship the original plan
When evidence changes, change the plan.

Examples:
- turn a strong campaign into an always-on campaign
- increase bids in proven regions
- narrow broad positioning into a stronger niche
- postpone a rebuild until preservation requirements are known
- pause an initiative until the formula is proven

The plan is subordinate to evidence.

## 20. Treat client context as part of the model
Performance data without business context can mislead.

Record:
- target customer
- service area
- average customer value
- sales capacity
- seasonality
- inventory / operational constraints
- brand / compliance constraints
- client feedback about lead quality

Optimization should reflect the actual business, not only platform metrics.

## Evidence confidence
Every extracted doctrine rule should carry one of:
- REPEATED_PATTERN — supported by multiple historical cases/eras
- CASE_PATTERN — clearly supported in one or a few cases
- CURRENT_HYPOTHESIS — plausible but not yet sufficiently evidenced
- RETIRED — historically used but no longer recommended

Do not preserve outdated tactics merely because they appear in old files.

## Continuous refinement loop
For each historical case:

`source evidence -> decision -> implementation -> outcome -> confidence -> doctrine update`

For each new venture:

`doctrine -> action -> measurement -> outcome -> new evidence`

The moat strengthens when VLA can distinguish enduring decision principles from tactics that were specific to a particular year, platform, client, or technology.
