# Content Authority Engine

## Objective
Build original, useful, commercially relevant content that humans trust and search/AI systems can understand, retrieve, and cite.

## Core principle
Write for humans first; structure facts so machines can understand them.

Do not create commodity filler at scale. Do not invent expertise, results, reviews, customers, certifications, statistics, or case studies.

## Founder knowledge interview
When original business knowledge is missing, ask only high-value questions such as:

- Why does this business exist?
- What is broken about the traditional alternative?
- What is the biggest misconception buyers have?
- When should someone not buy this?
- What determines price/value?
- What actually happens after purchase?
- What objections do buyers raise?
- What have you tried that failed?
- What evidence/results can be verified?
- What does an ideal customer look like?

Turn these answers into durable source material.

## Content Authority Stack
Build in this priority order:

1. Entity/About page
2. Product/service pages
3. How It Works / mechanism
4. Buyer Q&A / objections
5. Comparison/alternative content
6. Case studies with real evidence
7. Original data/research/calculators
8. Supporting editorial/blog content

## Mechanism content
Every venture should explain HOW it works in simple steps.

Prefer 3–7 explicit steps with clear inputs, process, and outputs.

## Structured inputs and outputs
Publish factual structured information where useful:

- industry/customer type
- geography
- product/service
- eligibility
- pricing model
- expected output/result type
- confirmation boundaries

## Buyer Q&A
Answer real purchase objections, not dictionary questions.

Examples:

- Why use this instead of the incumbent alternative?
- What exactly do I receive?
- What counts as qualified/completed?
- What happens if quality is poor?
- What is included/excluded?
- Who owns the data/account/work product?
- How is pricing determined?
- What risks or limitations should I know?

## Case study standard
Publish only when evidence exists. Include:

- customer/company context or appropriate anonymization
- industry
- geography
- baseline
- problem
- timeframe
- budget/resources
- strategy
- execution
- results
- CAC/CPA/revenue metrics when known
- what failed
- what worked
- limitations
- what would change next time

## Comparison content
Create substantive pages around real alternatives buyers evaluate.

Examples:

- brand vs traditional alternative
- managed service vs DIY
- pay-per-result vs retainer
- solution vs hiring internally
- solution vs data/list provider

Be fair. State where the alternative is better.

## Original research assets
Prioritize assets that compound authority:

- calculators
- pricing benchmarks
- market datasets
- surveys
- directories
- scorecards
- checklists
- methodology pages

## Blog/editorial rules
Each article must add at least one of:

- original experience
- original data
- strong synthesis
- practical framework
- unique comparison
- specific answer to commercial buyer intent

Do not publish generic articles simply to increase URL count.

## Content-only mode
When the user requests publication-ready content only:

- output only the finished content
- no preamble
- no explanation
- no postscript
- no SEO commentary unless requested

## Citation readiness checklist
A page is citation-ready when it has:

- clear entity/topic
- explicit definitions
- concrete mechanism
- verifiable facts
- useful specificity
- named limitations where relevant
- stable URL
- descriptive headings
- appropriate internal links
- visible author/business accountability when relevant
